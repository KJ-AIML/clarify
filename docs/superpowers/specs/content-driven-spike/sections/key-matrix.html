How to read this: the chip at the bottom powers one row wire at a time (orange). A pressed key closes its little switch, so its column wire answers (green). Click any key to press it.

The open switches on the powered row get power but have a gap: nothing reaches their columns. Only the pressed key closes the gap.

Count the wires

4 rows + 6 columns =10wires4 rows × 6 columns =24keys

4 + 6 = 10 wires watch 24 keys. Wires add up, keys multiply: every row crosses every column, and a key sits at each crossing.

A real keyboard does the same at full size: a couple of dozen wires watch over a hundred keys.

Bounce

Metal contacts bounce for a moment when they close. The chip waits about 5 ms for them to settle, then counts one press.

The chip never watches every key on its own wire. It powers one row at a time and listens for the column that answers: the row and the column together name the key.
