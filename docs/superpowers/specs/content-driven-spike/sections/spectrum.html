How to read this: the strip at the top is the air around your desk, from 2,400 to 2,483.5 MHz, cut into thin slices called channels. Below it, time runs down: each row is one report from the mouse, drawn on the channel it was sent on.

  • a report from the mouse
  • “got it”: the answer came back
  • lost: no answer
  • Wi-Fi sending at that moment

Who else makes noise here

  • Wi-Fi channels 1, 6 and 11, each about 20 MHz wide

  • Bluetooth 79 channels, 1 MHz each; hops up to 1,600 times a second and skips busy ones

  • Microwave ovens about 2,450 MHz, in the middle of the band

  • USB 3 ports noise in the same band; a receiver does better in a USB 2 port or on a short extension cable

This is a drawing of the idea, not a measurement: the rows and their channels are picked to show how it works.

A lost report is normal. The device hears no “got it”, so it sends the same report again a moment later, on another channel, and you never see it. You only notice when it keeps happening: then the arrow stutters or jumps.
